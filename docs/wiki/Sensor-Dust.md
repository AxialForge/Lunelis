# Sensor dust

Dust on a camera's sensor shows as the same small, soft, dark blot in the
same place on every photo - clearest at f/8 and narrower, on plain bright
areas like sky. **Sidebar > Keep safe > Sensor dust** finds it per camera and
heals it out of your photos, as edits.

## Finding it

Pick a camera (the list shows how many of its photos are at f/8 or
narrower) and click **Look for dust**. Lunelis reads those photos'
thumbnails, turns each back to how the sensor recorded it (so portrait shots
line up with landscape ones), and looks for small dark blots in smooth,
bright areas. A place counts as dust when at least half of the photos that
*could* show a blot there (plain and bright at that spot) do, in at least
five photos.

The map is the sensor as a rectangle:

- a **solid circle** per dust spot - the stronger the fill, the surer;
- a **dashed circle** for a spot that was there and then never again,
  although later photos would have shown it - the sensor was **cleaned**.
  The text says around when; dust that only appears after others were
  already there is called **new dust**.

The map is kept, so it's there next time; **Look for dust** again after new
photos come in.

## Healing it

**Heal on N photos** - N is every photo from that camera taken while the
spots were there (wide-open ones too: the dust is there even where it's hard
to see). **Show the photos** shows them in the library first.

Healing adds a **Heal** spot for each dust spot to those photos' edits
(see [Retouch](Editing.md#retouch)) - the files never change. Photos you've
rotated or flipped in Lunelis are left alone (the sensor's corners moved),
and the message says how many. **Undo the last heal** takes those spots off
again; spots you added yourself stay.
